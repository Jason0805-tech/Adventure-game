========================================
1. SANITY SYSTEM
========================================

Implement a complete Sanity system.

Base values:
- Maximum sanity: 100
- Initial sanity: 100
- Minimum sanity: 0

Sanity changes:
- Daytime exploration: no passive sanity loss.
- Nighttime darkness: -0.35 sanity/second.
- Protected light sources: prevent darkness-related sanity loss.
- Resting in a safe, illuminated camp: +0.25 sanity/second.
- Dangerous wildlife encounters: -5 sanity per encounter, with a cooldown.
- Severe hunger: additional gradual sanity loss.
- Selected existing food or rest interactions may restore sanity.

Avoid unlimited sanity restoration exploits.

SANITY LEVELS:

70-100: NORMAL
- No negative effects.

30-69: UNSTABLE
- Subtle screen-edge darkness.
- Occasional environmental visual distortions.
- Rare shadow hallucinations.
- Mild atmospheric effects.

0-29: CRITICAL
- Stronger but readable visual distortions.
- More frequent hallucinations.
- Actual hostile shadow creatures may appear.

SHADOW CREATURES:
- Only spawn at critical sanity.
- Maximum one active shadow creature near the player.
- Minimum 45-second spawn cooldown.
- Use existing combat and collision systems.
- Shadow creatures must be avoidable or defeatable.
- Despawn when sanity rises above 35 or conditions become safe.
- Do not use unavoidable damage or sudden unfair spawning.
- Reuse existing visual resources where possible.

VISUAL DESIGN:
- Add a Sanity indicator to the existing HUD.
- Keep the interface consistent with current visual styling.
- Support both desktop and mobile layouts.
- Avoid excessive screen effects that obstruct gameplay.

========================================
2. HUNGER REBALANCING
========================================

Current problem:
Hunger decreases too slowly and rarely affects survival.

Requirements:
- Preserve the existing hunger system.
- Rebalance passive hunger consumption to approximately 8 points/minute.
- Scale appropriately if the existing hunger range differs from 0-100.
- Existing food items must restore meaningful amounts of hunger.

Hunger consequences:
- Above 25%: normal gameplay.
- Below 25%: mild survival penalties.
- At 0: gradual health loss.
- Severe hunger also increases sanity loss.

Avoid instant death.

Food gathering, fishing, and food management should become meaningful survival activities.

Ensure sufficient accessible food resources exist so survival remains achievable.

========================================
3. WILDLIFE DIFFICULTY
========================================

Current problem:
Wolves and bears are too slow to threaten players.

Improve hostile wildlife behavior.

WOLVES:
- Slightly faster than normal player movement during pursuit.
- Maintain existing perception and warning behavior.
- Lose pursuit when the player escapes detection or reaches effective protection.

BEARS:
- Slower normal pursuit than wolves.
- Can perform short, faster charges.
- Charges must include visible warning behavior.
- Charges require cooldowns.

GENERAL RULES:
- Preserve existing animal movement and animation systems.
- Preserve species-specific behavior.
- Do not modify passive animals into aggressive enemies.
- Respect existing obstacles and collision.
- Prevent predators from spawning directly beside the player.
- Do not create unavoidable encounters.

Players must have realistic escape options through movement, obstacles, equipment, or environmental protection.

========================================
4. NIGHT SURVIVAL & CAMP SAFETY
========================================

Current problem:
The camp is completely safe, making preparation unnecessary.

Keep the camp useful, but remove unconditional safety.

CAMP:
- Preserve the existing permanent camp lantern.
- Light reduces wildlife aggression and visibility-related sanity loss.
- Camp protection must not completely disable survival mechanics.
- Hunger continues decreasing while resting.
- Nearby threats may still create pressure.
- Avoid unfair attacks immediately after spawning.

NIGHT DIFFICULTY:

NIGHT 1:
- Introduction to survival threats.
- Relatively forgiving enemy behavior.
- Allow players to learn preparation mechanics.

NIGHT 2:
- Increased predator activity.
- Greater importance of equipment and food.
- More meaningful environmental threats.

NIGHT 3:
- Highest survival pressure.
- Requires effective preparation and resource management.
- Difficult but achievable without perfect gameplay.

Preserve the existing day-night cycle duration unless a verified balancing issue requires adjustment.

Do not solve difficulty problems simply by spawning excessive enemies.

========================================
5. PLAYER PROGRESSION
========================================

Current problem:
Gathering resources, crafting weapons, and exploring are often optional and unrewarding.

Create meaningful progression using existing mechanics.

EARLY GAME:
- Basic resource gathering.
- Food acquisition.
- Simple tools and equipment.

MID GAME:
- Improved tools and weapons.
- More effective resource collection.
- Better preparation for dangerous encounters.

LATE GAME:
- Advanced survival equipment.
- Stronger defensive options.
- Preparation for the final night.

REQUIREMENTS:
- Preserve existing crafting recipes unless modification is necessary.
- Make equipment upgrades noticeably useful.
- Improved tools should increase gathering efficiency.
- Better weapons should meaningfully improve survival.
- Light sources should provide tactical advantages.
- Reward exploration with useful resources or opportunities.
- Ensure progression works within the existing three-night timeline.

Do NOT add an unnecessary XP or character-level system.

Progression should come primarily from equipment, resources, exploration, and preparation.

========================================
6. GAMEPLAY BALANCE
========================================

The intended gameplay loop is:

EXPLORE
→ GATHER RESOURCES
→ CRAFT EQUIPMENT
→ IMPROVE SURVIVAL CAPABILITY
→ SURVIVE THE NIGHT
→ EXPLORE FURTHER

Balance the systems together.

Avoid:
- Unfair difficulty spikes.
- Unavoidable enemy damage.
- Excessive hunger consumption.
- Uncontrollable sanity loss.
- Repetitive resource grinding.
- Equipment upgrades with no practical value.
- Excessive enemy spawning.

Each survival mechanic should create meaningful player decisions.

Players who prepare carefully should have a clear advantage over players who ignore survival mechanics.
